Famous Dostoevsky quotes

Fyodor Dostoevsky (1821–1881), one of the greatest novelists in world literature, explored human psychology, morality, faith, and the depths of suffering across masterpiece works like Crime and Punishment, The Brothers Karamazov, and Notes from Underground.

#On Love, Faith, and Morality
"If God does not exist, everything is permitted." — The Brothers Karamazov
"To love someone means to see them as God intended them to be."
"The mystery of human existence lies not in just staying alive, but in finding something to live for." — The Brothers Karamazov
"Love in action is a harsh and dreadful thing compared to love in dreams." — The Brothers Karamazov

#On Suffering, Beauty, and Hope
"Beauty will save the world." — The Idiot
"There is only one thing that I dread: not being worthy of my sufferings." — Quoted via Viktor Frankl, originating from Dostoevsky's reflections on prison life

"Pain and suffering are always inevitable for a large intelligence and a deep heart. The really great men must, I think, have great sadness on earth." — Crime and Punishment
"The darker the night, the brighter the stars; the deeper the grief, the closer is God!"

#On the Human Mind and Freedom
"To go wrong in one's own way is better than to go right in someone else's." — Crime and Punishment
"Man is a mystery. It needs to be unravelled, and if you spend your whole life unraveling it, don't say that you've wasted time." — Letter to his brother Mikhail (1839)
"It takes something more than intelligence to act intelligently." — Crime and Punishment
"I say let the world go to hell, but I should always have my tea." — Notes from Underground

#On Truth and Self-Deception
"Above all, don't lie to yourself. The man who lies to himself and listens to his own lie comes to a point that he cannot distinguish the truth within him, or around him, and so loses all respect for himself and for others." — The Brothers Karamazov
"Taking a new step, uttering a new word, is what people fear most." — Crime and Punishment
