##Japan property - Horiguchi Mia

Buying a home in Japan is exciting. You finally have your own place. No more paying rent. It feels like a dream.
But after living there for a while...
Many homeowners realize they didn't think about what comes after moving in. Here are some of the biggest reasons people regret buying a house in Japan.

1. The Location Looked Great... Until They Actually Lived There.
A 20-minute walk to the station doesn't sound bad.
It's raining until it starts raining.
Or it's 38°C in summer.
Or you're carrying groceries every day.
A house can be beautiful, but if the location doesn't fit your lifestyle, you'll feel it every single day.

2. Maintenance Costs Never End.
Many first-time buyers only think about the mortgage.
But houses need maintenance.
Roofs eventually need repairs.
Exterior walls need repainting.
Water heaters don't last forever.
Owning a home means you're responsible for every repair.

3. The House Was Bigger Than They Needed.
At first, a large house feels amazing.
A few years later...
You're cleaning rooms no one uses.
Paying higher utility bills.
Maintaining a garden you no longer enjoy.
Sometimes, bigger isn't better.

4. They Didn't Think About the Neighborhood.
A quiet neighborhood sounds perfect.
But...
Are there supermarkets nearby?
Hospitals?
Schools?
Is public transportation convenient?
Can you imagine living there 10 or 20 years from now?
The house is only part of the purchase.
The neighborhood matters just as much.

5. Property Values ​​Don't Always Go Up.
This surprises many foreigners.
In Japan, land often keeps its value in good locations.
But buildings usually lose value as they get older.
If you're buying purely as an investment, it's important to understand how the Japanese property market works before making a decision.

6. Their lifestyle changed.
People change.
No jobs.
Families grow.
Children move out.
What feels like the perfect house today may not fit your life 10 years later.

🏡The biggest lesson?
Don't buy a house because it's cheap.
Don't buy because it looks beautiful in photos.
Buy a house that fits your daily life.
The best home isn't always the biggest or the newest.
It's the one you'll still enjoy living in years from now.
As someone who has worked in Japanese real estate, I've seen buyers make great decisions and I've also seen people regret rushing into a purchase.
Take your time.
A house is one of the biggest investments you'll ever make.
