THE SHINKANSEN IS FAST…
BUT THESE 6 ALTERNATIVES
CAN SAVE YOU SERIOUS MONEY
The Shinkansen is one of the fastest, safest, and most comfortable ways to travel across Japan.
But speed comes at a price.
For students, families, long-term residents, and budget travellers, Shinkansen tickets can take a large part of the travel budget.
The good news is that Japan has several cheaper transport options.
They may take longer, but they can help you save thousands of yen, especially when you plan early.
Here are 6 alternatives worth checking before buying a Shinkansen ticket.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
OPTION ONE:
Daytime Highway Buses
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Highway buses connect major cities, regional towns, airports, theme parks, and tourist destinations across Japan.
They are usually slower than the Shinkansen, but fares can be much lower.
Popular routes include:
• Tokyo to Nagoya
• Tokyo to Osaka or Kyoto
• Tokyo to Sendai
• Osaka to Hiroshima
• Fukuoka to cities across Kyushu
Why they may save money:
• Lower fares than trains on many routes
• No transfer between stations
• Direct access to some tourist areas
• Different comfort levels and price ranges
• Advance-booking discounts may be available
What to check:
Bus travel can be affected by road traffic, weather, and holiday congestion.
Do not choose a tight connection immediately after your scheduled arrival time.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
OPTION TWO:
Overnight Buses
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
An overnight bus travels while you sleep and arrives the following morning.
This can save money in two ways:
• A lower transport fare
• One less night of hotel accommodation
Some buses offer:
• Reclining seats
• Privacy curtains
• Charging points
• Wi-Fi
• Toilets
• Separate seating options for women
However, comfort varies greatly by bus type and operator.
Best for:
Solo travellers, students, flexible travellers, and people who can sleep while travelling.
What to check:
Confirm the departure date carefully. A bus leaving shortly after midnight may be listed under the next calendar day.
Also check the arrival point, baggage allowance, seat type, and cancellation policy.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
OPTION THREE:
Low-Cost Domestic Flights
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
For long journeys, flying can sometimes cost less than taking the Shinkansen.
Budget airlines connect many Japanese cities, including routes to:
• Hokkaido
• Kyushu
• Okinawa
• Kansai
• Tohoku
• Shikoku
A flight can be especially useful when travelling between distant regions, such as Tokyo and Sapporo or Tokyo and Okinawa.
Why it may save money:
• Promotional fares can be very low
• Flying may be faster for long-distance routes
• Several regional airports are served by budget airlines
• Early booking can provide better prices
What to check:
The advertised fare may not include:
• Checked baggage
• Seat selection
• Payment fees
• Airport transport
• Food or drinks
• Ticket changes
Compare the complete door-to-door cost—not only the flight price.
A cheap flight from a distant airport may become less attractive after adding baggage and airport-transfer costs.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
OPTION FOUR:
Local and Rapid Trains
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
You do not always need the Shinkansen for short or medium-distance travel.
Japan’s local and rapid trains connect cities, suburbs, coastal areas, and regional destinations.
Rapid trains normally skip some stations without requiring the Shinkansen surcharge.
They can be useful for routes such as:
• Tokyo to Yokohama
• Osaka to Kyoto
• Kyoto to Nara
• Nagoya to nearby cities
• Fukuoka to regional destinations
• Travel within one prefecture or nearby regions
Why they may save money:
• No Shinkansen surcharge
• Frequent service in major urban areas
• Easy use with IC cards
• More stops near local attractions
• No seat reservation needed on many services
What to check:
Local travel can involve several transfers and may take much longer over large distances.
Before leaving, check the total journey time, final train, platform changes, and whether part of the route requires an extra express fee.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
OPTION FIVE:
Regional Transport Passes
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
A nationwide rail pass is not always the best value.
If your trip is focused on one region, a regional train, bus, or combined transport pass may cost less.
Different passes may cover areas such as:
• Tohoku
• Nagano and Niigata
• Hokuriku
• Kansai
• Chugoku
• Shikoku
• Kyushu
• Hokkaido
Some passes are available only to foreign visitors with specific eligibility, while others can also be used by residents.
Why they may save money:
• Unlimited travel within a defined area
• Better value for several day trips
• Some include trains, buses, or ferries
• Less need to buy separate tickets
• Regional passes may cost less than nationwide options
What to check:
A pass saves money only when the journeys you plan to take cost more than the pass itself.
Always confirm:
• Who can buy it
• The valid area
• The number of valid days
• Which trains are excluded
• Whether seat reservations are included
• Whether the days must be consecutive
Never buy a pass only because it sounds convenient.
Calculate first.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
OPTION SIX:
Ferries and Overnight Ships
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Japan is an island country with many ferry routes.
Ferries connect places such as:
• Honshu and Hokkaido
• Kansai and Kyushu
• Osaka and Shikoku
• Hiroshima and Matsuyama
• Kagoshima and nearby islands
• Okinawa and its smaller islands
Some long-distance ferries operate overnight and offer different accommodation classes.
Depending on the ship, facilities may include:
• Sleeping areas
• Private rooms
• Restaurants
• Public baths
• Showers
• Observation decks
Why they may save money:
• Transport and overnight accommodation can be combined
• Useful for travellers carrying bicycles or vehicles
• A relaxing alternative to busy airports and stations
• Beautiful sea views become part of the journey
What to check:
Ferries are slower and can be affected by bad weather.
Check the port location carefully because ferry terminals may be far from the city centre.
Also compare the cost of the basic sleeping area, private rooms, meals, and transport to and from the port.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
WHICH OPTION SHOULD YOU CHOOSE?
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Choose a daytime highway bus for a cheaper intercity journey.
Choose an overnight bus to save both transport and hotel costs.
Choose a domestic flight for very long distances.
Choose local or rapid trains for shorter regional journeys.
Choose a regional pass when planning several trips in one area.
Choose a ferry when you have more time and want the journey to become part of the experience.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
BEFORE YOU BOOK
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Compare:
• Total fare
• Travel time
• Baggage charges
• Airport or port transfers
• Seat comfort
• Cancellation conditions
• Hotel savings
• Arrival time and location
The cheapest ticket is not always the best value.
A journey that saves ¥3,000 but takes six additional hours may not suit every traveller.
Choose based on both your budget and your available time.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FINAL MESSAGE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
The Shinkansen is fast, comfortable, and convenient—but it is not always necessary.
Japan’s buses, domestic flights, local trains, regional passes, and ferries can help you travel farther without spending more than you need.
Before buying a Shinkansen ticket:
Compare the route.
Check the full cost.
Think about your time.

Sometimes travelling more slowly can save serious money—and show you a side of Japan that high-speed travel misses.
